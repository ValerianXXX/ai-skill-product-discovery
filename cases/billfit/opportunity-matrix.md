# Opportunity matrix

The project used two different scorecards. Publishing both is important: BillFit did not win the original broad screen.

## Pass A: broad pain and workflow screen

| Rank | Candidate | Final score | Decision after challenge |
|---:|---|---:|---|
| 1 | Customer-service context passport | 82 | Rejected as the first product: high general-AI substitutability and hidden integration complexity |
| 2 | Scam incident navigator | 74 | Deferred: strong need, but high trust burden and strong free official alternatives |
| 3 | Complex notice-to-action list | 72 | Rejected as too broad and too close to general document assistance |
| 4 | Senior phone screenshot coach | 70 | Rejected: image guidance is broadly available and risky in sensitive interfaces |
| 4 | School-message action summary | 70 | Deferred: high frequency but crowded supply and child-privacy concerns |

The full twelve-candidate screen is in [`data/initial-opportunity-screen.csv`](../../data/initial-opportunity-screen.csv). Scores are analyst judgments, not market-size estimates.

## What the first scorecard missed

“AI leverage” rewarded tasks where a model could produce an attractive output. It did not sufficiently distinguish:

- a model capability from a product capability;
- a saved prompt from a maintained data asset;
- a text demo from a complete workflow;
- a true unmet need from a hard-to-distribute integration.

The user challenge—“Can ordinary AI already do this?”—became a mandatory gate.

## Pass B: critical-data screen

| Candidate family | Score | Strongest property | Main constraint | Outcome |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Provider-specific rate fit and assistance | 93 | Exact current rates, user usage, deterministic math, and explicit gates | Provider-by-provider maintenance | Selected as BillFit |
| Product and replacement-part compatibility | 81 | Clear compatible/not-compatible value and deterministic mappings | Fragmented identifiers and data rights | Retain for future research |
| Local benefits and rebates | 76 | High-value eligibility decisions based on official rules | Very broad jurisdiction and refresh burden | Reframe to one program/provider first |
| Local consumer-rights deadlines | 70 | Current jurisdiction-specific rules matter | Legal-risk boundary and frequent rule changes | Defer |

The scoring inputs and reasoning are in [`data/critical-data-opportunity-screen.csv`](../../data/critical-data-opportunity-screen.csv).

## Why BillFit won

BillFit was not necessarily the largest possible market. It was the strongest **small, testable intersection** of:

- a meaningful decision;
- accessible official data;
- user-owned structured input;
- deterministic value;
- narrow scope;
- safe failure;
- in-AI distribution.

The choice optimized learning quality and reliability before national scale.
